- **Your idea in your own words:**

  When an internal procedure changes, the change owner may need to coordinate review, impacted teams, staff communication, and training. Missing details can lead to follow-up and rework before the updated procedure is ready. I propose a structured readiness checklist that helps the owner record affected teams, required control review, approvals, communication, and training before the change is marked ready for publication. It should show status and send reminders, but must not approve or publish a procedure automatically. Start with one low-risk procedure area and compare completion time and rework with its current process.

## Step 2 — Develop

- **Idea title:** Procedure Change Readiness Checklist
- **Summary:** Give procedure change owners a structured checklist to record impacted teams, control review, approvals, staff communication, and training. Show outstanding tasks and send reminders, while leaving approval and publication decisions with authorized people. Pilot with one low-risk procedure area and compare completion time and rework against the current process.
- **Problem statement:** Procedure updates can require input from several teams. If the change owner does not identify all required reviewers, communications, or training needs at the start, the change may need follow-up before it is ready. For sample testing only, assume a fictional eight-week review of 120 changes found 30 with at least one readiness-related follow-up. These figures are invented and must not be presented as measured bank data.
- **Proposed solution:** Add a role-restricted checklist to the existing procedure change workflow. The owner records the affected teams, control review requirement, accountable approver, communication plan, and training need. Each task has an owner and status, with reminders for overdue items. Keep approvals and publication in their existing controlled process. Do not automatically approve, publish, alter a procedure, or expose procedure content to users who lack access. Retain a timestamped history of checklist changes.
- **Expected benefits:** Reduce avoidable follow-up, make readiness responsibilities visible, and help owners identify blocked changes earlier. Measure the current and pilot completion time, follow-up count, overdue tasks, and missed communication or training requirements before deciding whether to expand.
- **Possible risks:** A checklist could omit a required control, show stale task status, or be treated as an approval. Restrict access by role, have the process owner approve checklist fields, label the checklist as readiness tracking rather than approval, preserve the existing authorization steps, and review audit history and exceptions during the pilot.

## Step 3 — Questions reviewers may ask

These answers are collected before the Pass or fail step. The backend includes them in the pinned AI validation input as reviewer evidence; they can inform the score and its explanations.

- **Who benefits?** Procedure change owners and the teams asked to review or prepare for a change. Staff who need to follow the updated procedure may benefit from more consistent communication and training readiness.
- **What happens today?** The change owner coordinates reviews and readiness tasks through the current procedure change process. The precise handoffs, tools, and baseline follow-up rate should be confirmed with the selected process owner before a pilot.
- **What is needed?** A process owner to define required checklist fields; agreement on the pilot scope; role-based access to the relevant change record; named owners for review, communication, and training tasks; and information-security, records, and control review before implementation.
- **What could go wrong?** The checklist may be incomplete or out of date, a user may mistake task completion for formal approval, or reminders may reach the wrong person. Keep formal approvals in the existing workflow, clearly distinguish readiness from authorization, test access rules, and provide an exception path.
- **How is success measured?** Compare median time from change initiation to readiness, the number of follow-up loops, overdue tasks, and missed required communication or training items with a pre-pilot baseline. Have reviewers check a sample of changes and record any access or control incidents.
